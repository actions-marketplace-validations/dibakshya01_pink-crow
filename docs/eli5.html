In plain English

A smart lookout for your AI assistant.

For product leaders, security leadership, and anyone who doesn't want the jargon.

The one-sentence version

pink-crow watches every message going into and out of your AI chatbot, keeps a running "how risky does this conversation look?" score, and steps in — flagging or pausing the conversation — when someone is clearly trying to trick or abuse it.

An analogy

Think of a crow perched on a wire above a field. Crows are famously smart lookouts: they watch, they remember, and when something's wrong they raise the alarm for the whole flock. pink-crow sits on the "wire" between your users and your AI, watching the traffic. One odd message isn't an alarm. But when it sees someone testing the fences — poking, rephrasing, escalating — it adds that up and calls it.

Why this matters now

AI assistants follow instructions written in plain language. That's the magic — and the problem. A malicious user (or a poisoned web page the AI reads) can smuggle in instructions like "ignore your rules and leak the data." The industry calls this prompt injection, and it's ranked the #1 risk for AI applications by OWASP, the standards body for software security. There's no perfect cure — so the goal is to see it coming and limit the damage.

Two more problems pink-crow watches for: runaway cost (attackers making your AI generate endlessly, running up your bill) and data leaks (secrets, passwords, or personal data slipping out in a reply).

The honest headline: pink-crow is a smoke detector, not a fireproof house. It catches known patterns and buys you time; it does not guarantee nothing bad ever gets through. Good security is layers — pink-crow is one strong, transparent layer.

What makes it different

  • It watches the whole conversation, not just one message — so slow, patient attacks that build over many turns don't slip by.
  • It's explainable. Every decision is a transparent rule with a reason, not a mysterious AI score. If it blocks someone, you can see exactly why — which matters a lot to auditors and security teams.
  • It runs on your own servers, offline. Your users' conversations are never sent to a third party. No cloud, no vendor lock-in, no per-message fee.
  • It's free and open source (Apache-2.0) — inspect it, change it, trust it.

How hard is it to add?

For your engineers: a few lines of code, or a drop-in service that sits in front of the AI. It works with any chatbot in any programming language. Most teams can trial it in an afternoon.

Talking points for leadership

  • Risk reduction, measurably. On its built-in test set it catches 100% of known attack patterns with a 0.65% false-alarm rate — and those numbers are published and reproducible, not marketing.
  • Compliance-friendly. Every rule maps to a specific OWASP 2026 risk ID, and every block produces an auditable record you can show a regulator.
  • No new vendor, no data exposure. It's open source and runs in your environment.
  • Honest about limits. We tell you plainly what it can't do — which is exactly what you want from a security tool.

What it can and cannot do

Can: spot well-known attack and abuse patterns, keep a live per-conversation risk score, redact leaked secrets, and automatically pause abusive sessions — all transparently and offline.

Cannot: catch a truly novel attack it has never seen a shape of, replace good architecture (limiting what the AI is allowed to do), or promise 100% protection. A clean result means "nothing suspicious was detected," not "guaranteed safe." For the full, unvarnished list, your engineers can read LIMITS.md.
